1. The question

How much sediment organic carbon is in this (synthetic) eelgrass area, and how precisely do the cores pin down the mean?

3. The estimate — 0–30 cm, fully measured

Sediment organic carbon only. This is a stock — the carbon in the sediment now — not a rate of accumulation and not removal of carbon from the atmosphere. Every core reached 30 cm, so this figure is measured: nothing below the base of any core is in it.

Mean 26.7 Mg C/ha over 5.6 ha: about 151 Mg C in the top 30 cm of sediment (hypothetical area). 90% interval: 21.5–31.9 Mg C/ha, or 121–180 Mg C in total (±19% of the mean; the planning target was ±20%, which was met).

What the interval includes. It describes how much the mean could differ because a different set of sampling units might have been drawn. The area was treated as continuous (no finite-population correction). It does not include laboratory error, error in the LOI-to-carbon conversion, error in any estimated part below the cores, error in the boundary or stratum areas, or bias from cores that did not recover the full profile.

The map shows the reporting area, where the cores were taken and one value per area or stratum. Nothing is mapped between the points: the estimate is an area average, not a map of where the carbon is.

Depths not reported. Following Janousek et al. (2025), a stock is reported to 30 cm only for cores at least 20 cm long, to 50 cm for cores at least 35 cm and to 100 cm for cores at least 75 cm:

  • 0–50 cm — needs every core to reach 35 cm; too short: SYN-S2.
  • 0–100 cm — needs every core to reach 75 cm; too short: SYN-D1, SYN-D2, SYN-D3a, SYN-D3b, SYN-D4, SYN-D5, SYN-D6, SYN-S1, SYN-S2, SYN-S3, SYN-S4, SYN-S5.

5. How was depth estimated?

Nothing in the estimate above was extrapolated. This section shows how a deeper figure would be built, for reference.

  1. The same checked core stocks as Option A. Every slice passed the workbook’s checks; cores that did not are left out (section 7).
  2. A carbon curve for each core, in 1 cm steps. Inside the measured core each cm takes its slice’s carbon, so the curve adds up to exactly the measured stock. Below the core, carbon density is estimated — first with a decay curve fitted to that core, then one fitted to all cores in its stratum, and if neither works, the mean of the deepest 5 cm held constant.

6. Notes from the calculation

  • Unsampled strata excluded: channel_edge (0.59 ha). The estimate and total cover the sampled strata only.
  • The interval describes sampling variation among sampling units only. It does not include laboratory error, uncertainty in the LOI-to-carbon conversion, uncertainty in the estimated (extrapolated) part of each core, error in the boundary or stratum areas, or bias from cores that did not recover the full profile.

7. Missing data, exclusions and limitations

  • No cores were left out by the checks.
  • 0.59 ha in unsampled strata is excluded: the total covers 5.64 ha, not the whole boundary.
  • The boundary is hypothetical, so the area and total describe no real place.

8. What this supports, and what would help

Supports: an estimate of the mean and total sediment organic carbon stock in the sampled area to 30 cm, with an interval for sampling variation.

Does not support: a map of where carbon is within the area, a rate of accumulation, or removal of carbon from the atmosphere. The interval leaves out the sources listed in section 3.

What would help: cores that reach deeper standard depths, elemental organic carbon on a subset of slices to check the LOI conversion, and measured stratum areas.
